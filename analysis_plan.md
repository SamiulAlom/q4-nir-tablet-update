## 1. Working title
"How many reference assays after a change? Updating near-infrared tablet-content models across instruments and manufacturing scales."
Fixed at the draft stage. The word "batch" is used only if Dyrby 2002 confirms that the 31 tablet groups of the Copenhagen set are batches (`data_sources.md`, `[CHECK]`).

## 2. Question
A calibration that predicts the active-ingredient content of a tablet from its near-infrared spectrum is built in one condition and used in another: a second spectrometer, or a larger manufacturing scale. Each labelled tablet from the new condition costs a destructive reference assay.

**Primary question.** With a small number *n* of labelled tablets from the new condition, which model family predicts the remaining new-condition tablets best when every family is updated by the same rule, and how does the error fall as *n* grows?

**Secondary questions.**
1. How large is the loss with no update (n = 0), against the error each family shows under a random split? (the opening table)
2. How many labelled tablets does each family need to come back within 10% of its own source-condition error?
3. Within each family, does its own update protocol do better than the simple slope-and-bias correction? (a comparison of whole update protocols, not of families)

This is a comparison of existing methods under one protocol. No new model is proposed, and the paper will not claim one.

- Population: pharmaceutical tablets of two public datasets. Index: updated machine-learning calibrations. Comparator: partial least squares (PLS) with the same update; the other classical transfer methods of section 5 are shown beside it. Outcome: prediction error of content on held-out new-condition tablets.

## 3. Data
Sources, hashes and verified facts: `data_sources.md`. Nothing is removed as an outlier in the main analysis.

**Scenario I, instrument change (IDRC 2002 shootout; holds the pre-specified primary comparison).**
- Target value: assay. Spectra: all 650 variables.
- Source condition: instrument 1. New condition: instrument 2. The reverse direction is a secondary analysis.
- Source training set: the organisers' calibration and validation parts together (195 tablets), source-instrument spectra.
- New-condition tablets: the organisers' test part (460 tablets), new-instrument spectra. No test-part tablet is used to build or tune a source model.
- One repetition: the 460 are split at random into an update pool of 100 and an evaluation set of 360; the pool is put in random order and the first n = 5, 10, 20, 40, 80 tablets are the labelled update set (nested). n = 0 is the model with no update. **R = 200 repetitions**, the same splits for every method (seed list fixed in notebook 01 and frozen in a file before any model is fitted). Notebook 01 gives every tablet one identifier shared by its two spectra, and asserts row by row in every repetition that training, update and evaluation tablets are disjoint. **The pairing of the two instruments' spectra rests on the file's layout** (row i of each instrument in a part; one reference table per part serves both; the file has no other identifier), so it cannot be proved from the file. Notebook 01 checks it on the spectra alone: per part, the share of tablets whose own partner is among the 5 most similar rows in both directions must be at least 0.5 and at least 5 times the share under two wrong pairings (random shuffle, neighbouring rows swapped). If a part fails, the methods that need paired spectra (S, S+U1 and reference level (a)) are dropped by a recorded deviation before notebook 02; the main comparison does not need the pairing. The repetitions are a frozen list in a file, bound by a digest to the arrays they index. Evaluation spectra enter only prediction and scoring.

**Scenario II, manufacturing-scale change with held-out tablet groups (Copenhagen set).**
- Target value: active content (% w/w). Spectra: all 404 variables.
- Source condition: scale codes 0 and 1 (19 groups, 190 tablets). New condition: scale code 2 (12 groups, 120 tablets, three groups per tablet type).
- One repetition: one new-condition group per type is drawn as the update pool (4 groups, 40 tablets); the other 8 groups (80 tablets) are the evaluation set, so **no evaluation tablet shares a group with an update tablet or a training tablet**. The update set takes 1, 2, 4 or 10 tablets from each pool group (n = 4, 8, 16, 40, nested, random order within group). All 3⁴ = 81 choices of pool groups are used, with 3 random orders each: **R = 243**. Fits that do not depend on the order (n = 0 and n = 40) are computed once per choice of pool groups, with the update tablets in sorted row order and one fit seed per choice.
- Tuning inside the source condition uses group-held-out folds (all tablets of a group in one fold).

## 4. Models and how each is tuned
All tuning uses source-condition data only, by cross-validation (Scenario I: 5-fold, repeated 3 times; Scenario II: group-held-out 5-fold). The preprocessing is chosen per family from one fixed list: none; standard normal variate (SNV); Savitzky-Golay first derivative (window 15, order 2); Savitzky-Golay second derivative (window 15, order 2); SNV then first derivative. Preprocessing is per spectrum, so it needs no fitting on new-condition data.

| Family | Model | Tuned on source data |
|---|---|---|
| PLS (classical reference) | PLS regression, mean-centred | preprocessing; 1 to 15 components (smallest number within one standard error of the best) |
| Boosted trees | XGBoost regressor on the preprocessed spectrum | preprocessing; depth {2, 3, 4}, learning rate {0.03, 0.1}, up to 500 trees with early stopping, column subsample 0.3 |
| 1D CNN | three convolution blocks (16, 32, 64 filters, kernel 7, ELU, max-pool 2, no batch normalisation), global average pooling, dense 32, linear output; target standardised; Adam, weight decay 1e-4; training augmentation by random offset, slope and scale of the spectrum | preprocessing; learning rate {1e-3, 3e-4}; epochs by early stopping on a 20% inner split; 3 seeds |
| Tabular foundation model | TabPFN regressor, used in context with no training; shootout spectra averaged in pairs of neighbouring variables (325 features) for this family only, to stay below the 500 features above which the model subsamples features per estimator (version 2 weights, decision 002; 650 features are within its overall limit) | preprocessing only |

Every family gets the same tuning information and no family sees new-condition data during tuning. Centring, target scaling and early stopping are fitted inside each training fold. PLS components: the cross-validated RMSE and its standard error over folds are computed per number of components; the choice is the smallest number whose RMSE is at most the minimum plus one standard error (ties go to fewer components). The foundation model's package version, weight file (by hash) and feature handling are pinned in notebook 02 before the pilot run and written to `outputs/`.

## 5. Update methods (what is done with the n labelled new-condition tablets)
| Code | Update | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| U0 | none | all families |
| U1 | slope-and-bias correction: a straight line fitted from the model's predictions for the n tablets to their reference values (bias only when n < 8) | all families |
| U2 | the family's own update: PLS and boosted trees are refitted on source plus update tablets, each update tablet counted 5 times, hyperparameters unchanged; the CNN is fine-tuned on all layers for 200 steps at learning rate 1e-4, each step on the update tablets plus an equal number of random source tablets; the foundation model receives source plus update tablets as its context | all families |
| T | PLS only: Tikhonov-type updating. The regression vector is re-estimated from the n update tablets with a ridge penalty on its distance from the source vector (penalty λ), intercept re-estimated | both scenarios, at every n |
| S, S+U1 (**dropped, deviation 1**) | PLS only: piecewise direct standardisation of new-instrument spectra to the source instrument (window 11), fitted on the same n tablets' paired spectra without reference values, then the source PLS model (S); the same followed by slope-and-bias correction (S+U1) | Scenario I only, at every n. Marked in every table as needing the update tablets to be measured again on the old instrument, which is not always possible |

**How the update settings are set (changed in v1.1).** Each update has one setting with a grid of three values: copies of each update tablet {1, 5, 20} (PLS and boosted-tree refits), fine-tuning steps {50, 200, 800} at learning rate 1e-4 (CNN), context = source plus update tablets with update tablets repeated {1, 5, 20} times (foundation model), λ at {0.1, 1, 10} times the mean squared norm of the update spectra (T). The PDS window stays 11.
- *Scenario II:* the value is chosen without new-condition data, on pseudo-transfer inside the source condition: models built on scale code 0 are updated with tablets of scale code 1 and scored on other code-1 groups (27 choices of pool groups, drawn with a fixed seed from the 81), and the reverse (all 8 choices; scale code 0 has a single type-1 group, which is therefore always in the pool, so that direction scores types 2-4 only). The mean RMSE at n = 8 is taken per direction and the two means are averaged with equal weight; the value with the lowest average is used at every n; a tie goes to the middle value, then to the smaller. Preprocessing and base settings for these pseudo-source models are chosen again inside the pseudo-source scale alone, by group-held-out folds, so that no group of the other scale takes part.
- *Scenario I:* the source condition holds one instrument only, so no pseudo-transfer exists. The middle value is the main setting, fixed here, and the results for all three values are reported in full (no best value is picked).

Because U2 differs in kind between families, a U2 result is always described as the result of that family's update protocol.

*Deviation 1 replaces level (a) for Scenario I: see section 10.* Reference levels shown beside the curves, per family: (a) the source model's error on the same evaluation tablets measured in the source condition (Scenario I; possible because every tablet was measured on both instruments), or its group-held-out cross-validation error in the source condition (Scenario II); (b) Scenario I only: the family trained on the 195 training tablets as measured on the new instrument.

## 6. Outcomes
- **Primary outcome:** root-mean-square error (RMSE) of the predicted content on the evaluation tablets.
- **Primary comparison (pre-specified, descriptive):** Scenario I, instrument 1 to 2, **n = 10**, the same update for every family (U1): each of the three machine-learning families against PLS. Reported as the paired RMSE ratio per repetition.
- Shown beside it at every n: PLS with T (the classical transfer method; S and S+U1 were dropped by deviation 1), and each family's U2.
- Secondary: RMSE at every n (the curves); bias (mean error); recovery budget, Scenario I: per repetition, the ratio of a method's RMSE to its family's reference level (a) (after deviation 1: the fixed benchmark defined in section 10, not the same tablets); the budget is the first n on the grid whose median ratio over repetitions is at most 1.10, reported as "not reached" if none qualifies, with a flag if a larger n later exceeds 1.10; Scenario II: the same against the source cross-validation error, called a benchmark, not a recovery level; ratio of U0 error to random-split cross-validation error (opening table); the same for Scenario II and for the reverse instrument direction.
- Scenario II also reports the error separately for evaluation tablets whose content lies inside the source-condition range and above its largest value (extrapolation), with their counts.
- Descriptive, for the optical side of the paper: mean and first-derivative difference spectra between the two instruments (same tablets) and between scale codes, by wavelength region. No model is involved.

## 7. Statistical analysis
- **Variation over repetitions** (which tablets happened to be assayed) is shown as the median and the 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles over the R repetitions. It is described as resampling variability of the update set, not as a confidence interval for a population.
- **No significance test (changed in v1.1).** Tablets evaluated under one shared update fit have dependent errors, and the shootout's batches are unknown, so neither a tablet-level bootstrap nor a sign-flip test has a defensible dependence structure. Every comparison is reported as the paired RMSE ratio per repetition (the splits are shared): median, 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles, and the share of repetitions in which the method has the lower error. These describe this dataset under redrawn update sets. For the CNN the three seeds' RMSEs are averaged per repetition.
- **Scenario II is descriptive too.** Eight evaluation groups are too few clusters for a trustworthy test. Reported: curves with repetition percentiles, per-group errors, and the range of each comparison when one evaluation group at a time is left out. No p-value anywhere in the paper. Weight and hardness are not analysed.
- **Cost rule.** One full repetition of every method is timed first. If the whole design would need more than 12 hours on this PC, R is halved for every method in both scenarios (Scenario I: repetitions 0 to 99 of the frozen list; Scenario II: all 81 choices of pool groups are kept and the orders are cut within every choice, to orders 0 and 1 if the estimate is above 12 hours and to order 0 if it is still above 12 hours after that; the split file itself is not changed), decided from the timing alone and recorded in section 10.

## 8. Sensitivity and exploratory analyses (labelled as such in the paper)
- PLS and boosted trees on the pair-averaged 325 shootout features, to see what the averaging used for the foundation model costs.
- Foundation model with the update tablets alone as context, for n ≥ 20.
- Shootout with the organisers' validation part left out of training.
- If time is short these are cut in this order: last first.

## 9. What will not be claimed
- That any family is best for near-infrared calibration in general: two products, two kinds of change.
- Anything about batches in the shootout data (it has no batch label), or that its tablets are independent; the paper says so.
- That any difference is statistically established: the paper reports variability over redrawn update sets on two fixed datasets and no test.
- That one family beats another when the two were updated by different protocols (U2): those are protocol comparisons under the settings of section 5.
- Regulatory suitability of any model, or a recommended number of assays for other products.
- That the foundation model "needs no training data": it is given the source tablets as context.

- **Version 1.5, clarifications of section 5 (2026-11-23, written with notebook 03 and before any update method was run on any tablet).** Section 5 left these open; they are fixed here:
  - *No update (U0).* Each source model predicts all new-condition tablets of a scenario once; a repetition's update and evaluation predictions are taken from that vector. U1 is fitted on the update tablets' part of it and applied to the evaluation tablets' part. For the CNN, U1 is fitted per seed.
